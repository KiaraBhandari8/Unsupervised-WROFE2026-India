# Why we chose each component 

| **Component**                                                    | **Picture**                                                                                                                                                      | **Why We Chose It**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Raspberry Pi 5 (Cooling Fan + SD Card)**                       | <img width="100" height="67" alt="Raspberry Pi 5" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/e4878e2a-ee42-4cbb-9076-6eee5a91137b" />                       | We chose the Raspberry Pi 5 as the **main computing unit** of the robot because it has enough processing power to handle computationally demanding tasks such as computer vision, LiDAR data processing, navigation, and decision-making simultaneously. Its GPIO and communication interfaces also allow it to communicate with the other hardware components. The cooling fan helps prevent overheating during continuous operation, while the SD card provides storage for the operating system, programs, and collected data. |
| **Raspberry Pi Camera Module 3 Wide**                            | <img width="62" height="100" alt="Raspberry Pi Camera Module 3 Wide" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/e67ea9f3-6510-4a26-8285-e8302a622a39" />    | We chose the Raspberry Pi Camera Module 3 Wide as the robot's primary **vision sensor** because its wide-angle field of view allows the robot to capture more of the track and surrounding environment. This is useful for identifying the track, detecting visual features, and estimating the robot's position relative to the track. Its compatibility with the Raspberry Pi also makes it suitable for real-time image processing.                                                                                            |
| **ESP32 Development Board**                                      | <img width="100" height="100" alt="ESP32 Development Board" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/d5deb83d-41c4-404e-a340-20d3ba21cfaf" />             | We chose the ESP32 as the robot's **low-level control unit** because it can respond quickly to motor and steering commands while the Raspberry Pi focuses on higher-level processing. It provides multiple GPIO and communication interfaces and can communicate with the Raspberry Pi through serial communication. This separation of responsibilities makes the overall control system more reliable and responsive.                                                                                                           |
| **YDLidar T-Mini Plus LiDAR**                                    | <img width="100" height="100" alt="YDLidar T-Mini Plus LiDAR" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/0a3fde64-41e3-4732-825b-1a5cbe024fa0" />           | We chose the YDLidar T-Mini Plus to provide **360° distance measurements** around the robot. Unlike a camera, LiDAR provides direct distance information, which is useful for detecting obstacles and determining the available space around the robot. Its scanning capability allows our navigation system to analyse the surroundings and make decisions for obstacle avoidance and movement.                                                                                                                                  |
| **PCA9685 16-Channel PWM Servo Driver**                          | <img width="100" height="100" alt="PCA9685 16-Channel PWM Servo Driver" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/f667c49a-ed28-44c9-a9d9-43d50fd700eb" /> | We chose the PCA9685 to provide **precise and stable PWM control** for the steering servo. It can generate multiple PWM signals independently, allowing servo control without placing unnecessary load on the ESP32's hardware resources. Using a dedicated PWM controller also gives us consistent servo positioning, which is important for accurate steering.                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **TB6612FNG Dual Motor Driver**                                  | <img width="100" height="100" alt="TB6612FNG Dual Motor Driver" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/4f52e1c2-4c8d-45e9-8cb3-94717e99e67c" />         | We chose the TB6612FNG because it can control **two DC motors independently**, including their direction and speed. Its PWM-based speed control allows us to adjust the motor output according to the robot's movement requirements. It is also compact and efficient, making it suitable for a small autonomous robot where both space and power efficiency are important.                                                                                                                                                       |
| **GY-87 10-DOF Multi-Sensor IMU Module**                         | <img width="100" height="100" alt="GY-87 IMU Module" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/06444ccd-9718-44a6-9ecc-b5ebb76ae830" />                    | We chose the GY-87 to provide additional **motion and orientation information** about the robot. Its accelerometer and gyroscope can be used to detect changes in movement and orientation, while the other onboard sensors provide additional environmental and motion data. This information can complement the camera and LiDAR and can be useful for analysing the robot's movement and maintaining stable operation.                                                                                                         |
| **Silicon Labs CP2102 USB-to-UART Bridge**                       | <img width="100" height="100" alt="CP2102 USB-to-UART Bridge" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/51b86460-cb8d-4ee2-b4d7-cae04b5f1223" />           | We chose the CP2102 USB-to-UART bridge to provide a reliable **serial communication interface** between a computer and the ESP32. It simplifies programming, firmware uploading, and debugging by allowing serial data to be transmitted through USB. This makes development and troubleshooting of the ESP32 control system easier.                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| **HC-SR04 Ultrasonic Sensor**                                    | <img width="100" height="100" alt="HC-SR04 Ultrasonic Sensor" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/6b3e06b6-78e6-4789-aa50-27cb1e9129cb" />           | We chose the HC-SR04 as an additional **short-range distance sensor** to provide another source of obstacle detection. It is simple to interface with the ESP32 and can detect objects at close distances. Using it alongside the LiDAR provides an additional sensing layer that can help detect obstacles in situations where a second distance measurement is useful.                                                                                                                                                          |
| **MG996 Servo Motor**                                            | <img width="100" height="100" alt="MG996 Servo Motor" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/4e8ed4f9-2cff-482b-ac7c-e2f42677f6e7" />                   | We chose the MG996 servo motor for the **steering mechanism** because it provides relatively high torque and a wide range of angular movement. Steering requires the servo to consistently hold the required position against mechanical resistance, so a higher-torque servo is appropriate for this application. Its commonly supported control interface also makes it straightforward to operate using the PCA9685.                                                                                                           |
| **N20 200 rpm Gear Motors**                                      | <img width="100" height="100" alt="N20 Gear Motors" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/79ed59ec-01e8-4c4c-be57-d74fc5afe898" />                     | We chose the N20 200 rpm gear motors because their **compact size and geared output** provide a suitable combination of speed and torque for our robot. The gear reduction allows the motors to produce enough torque to move the robot while maintaining a manageable rotational speed. Their small form factor also makes them suitable for the limited space available within the chassis.                                                                                                                                     |
| **LiPo 3S 11.1V 2200 mAh Battery**                               | <img width="100" height="100" alt="LiPo 3S 11.1V 2200mAh Battery" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/975ba1bc-026f-4a03-b727-340d5b2d7c12" />       | We chose a 3S 11.1V 2200 mAh LiPo battery as the main power source because it provides a good **balance between voltage, capacity, weight, and size**. The battery can supply the current required by the motors while also providing enough stored energy for the robot's electronics. Its high energy density helps keep the overall robot lightweight while still providing sufficient runtime.                                                                                                                                |
| **LM2596 Step-Down Buck Converter (ESP32)**                      | <img width="100" height="100" alt="LM2596 Buck Converter" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/3a850fdf-1e0b-4753-a041-a333159d0240" />               | We chose the LM2596 buck converter to safely **reduce the battery voltage to a suitable regulated voltage** for the ESP32 and its associated electronics. Since the 3S LiPo battery provides a higher voltage than the ESP32 requires, a step-down converter is necessary. The adjustable output also allows the supply voltage to be set according to the requirements of the connected electronics.                                                                                                                             |
| **XY-3606 DC-DC Step-Down Buck Converter Module (Raspberry Pi)** | <img width="100" height="100" alt="XY-3606 Buck Converter" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/8af8d885-89c7-4b68-9acc-c355d38f7582" />              | We chose the XY-3606 buck converter to provide a **regulated power supply for the Raspberry Pi 5**. It converts the higher voltage from the LiPo battery into the voltage required by the Raspberry Pi while maintaining a stable supply. This helps prevent voltage-related instability during operation, especially when the motors are drawing significant current from the battery.                                                                                                                                           |
| **RC Car Rear Differential**                                     | <img width="100" height="100" alt="RC Car Rear Differential" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/783b6d71-c0a7-4619-8c8f-c254a46ddcd5" />            | We chose an RC car rear differential as part of the robot's **mechanical drive system** because it distributes power between the two rear wheels while allowing them to rotate at different speeds. This is particularly useful when the robot turns, as the inner and outer wheels travel different distances. The differential therefore provides smoother and more mechanically appropriate turning behaviour than directly forcing both wheels to rotate at the same speed.                                                   |
| **N20 Wheels**                                                   | <img width="100" height="100" alt="N20 Wheels" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/e54c118c-2956-4393-8d43-4b2afd229bf5" />                          | We chose N20 wheels because they are **compact, lightweight, and mechanically compatible with the N20 gear motors** used in our drive system. Their dimensions are suitable for our chassis and provide the required ground clearance while keeping the robot compact. They also work with our rear differential setup, allowing the drive system to transfer motor power effectively to the wheels.                                                                                                                              |
| **Lazy Susan Turntable Bearings**                                | <img width="100" height="100" alt="Lazy Susan Turntable Bearing" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/236d9dd5-82f3-4874-a29f-3ce52fc81cbf" />        | We chose Lazy Susan turntable bearings to provide **smooth rotational movement while supporting the mechanical load** of the rotating assembly. Their low-friction design reduces resistance during rotation and helps maintain consistent movement. They also provide a stable mechanical mounting point, making them useful for components that need to rotate while remaining securely supported.                                                                                                                              |
